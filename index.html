About

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Computer Science at Amherst College. I received my Ph.D. in Electrical and Computer Engineering from Princeton University, where I worked with Professor Jennifer Rexford and Professor Maria Apostolaki to design high-performance, Internet-native systems for network security and privacy. My research lies at the intersection of software-defined networking, systems, and security, leveraging programmable data planes to make today's networks faster to defend and harder to attack.

At Amherst, I am building a student-centered research group focused on practical network security, programmable networked systems, and emerging threats at the intersection of AI, mobile, and edge computing. Please reach out if you are interested in my work or in research opportunities.

Research Interests

  • Network Security & Privacy — Practical, deployable systems that defend real-world networks at scale without performance cost.
  • Programmable Data Planes — Hardware–software co-design with P4 and eBPF to push security functions into the network itself.
  • Cooperative Network Mechanisms — Incentive-aligned protocols that let mutually-interested networks collectively protect Internet paths.
