About

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Computer Science at Amherst College. I received my Ph.D. in Electrical and Computer Engineering from Princeton University, where I worked with Professor Jennifer Rexford and Professor Maria Apostolaki to design high-performance, Internet-native systems for network security and privacy. My research lies at the intersection of software-defined networking, systems, and security, leveraging programmable data planes across hardware and software to make today's networks safer and smarter.

My work has been recognized with competitive funding, high-profile awards, and patents. SmartCookie [USENIX Security 2024] was supported by three years of NSF GRFP funding and is currently patent-pending; Tango [NSDI 2024] received the Applied Networking Research Prize from IETF/IRTF; and PraxiGuard secured Princeton's Wallace Memorial Fellowship.

At Amherst, I am building a student-centered research group focused on practical network security and privacy, programmable networked systems, and emerging networking challenges in AI, mobile, and edge environments. Please reach out if you are interested in my work or research opportunities.

Research Interests

  • Network Security & Privacy — Practical systems that deliver security and privacy on real-world networks without compromising performance.
  • Programmable Data Planes — Hardware–software co-design using P4, eBPF, and programmable switches to push defenses into the network itself.
  • Cooperative Network Mechanisms — Incentive-aligned collaborative protocols that enable deployable, collective protections at Internet scale.
