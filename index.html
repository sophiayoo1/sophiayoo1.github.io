About

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Computer Science at Amherst College. I received my Ph.D. in Electrical and Computer Engineering from Princeton University in 2026, where my dissertation — Cooperative Network Systems for Protecting Internet Users — was advised by Jennifer Rexford and Maria Apostolaki. Prior to Princeton, I earned a B.S. in Electrical Engineering summa cum laude from Temple University (2020), with a Computer Engineering concentration and minors in Computer Science and Music.

My research builds high-performance, Internet-native systems for network security and privacy at the intersection of software-defined networking, programmable data planes, and systems security. I leverage hardware–software co-design (P4, eBPF) to push defenses into the network itself, and design cooperative protocols that let mutually-interested networks protect Internet paths they cannot secure alone.

At Amherst, I am building a student-centered research group focused on practical network security, programmable networked systems, and emerging threats in AI, mobile, and edge environments. Please reach out if you are interested in my work or in research opportunities.

Research Interests

  • Network Security & Privacy — Practical, deployable systems that defend real-world networks at scale without performance cost.
  • Programmable Data Planes — Hardware–software co-design with P4 and eBPF to push security functions into the network itself.
  • Cooperative Network Mechanisms — Incentive-aligned protocols that let mutually-interested networks collectively protect Internet paths.
